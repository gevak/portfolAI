The website is built as an immersive, single-page CSS 3D environment that simulates walking through a modernist, minimalist art gallery. By using a deep CSS `perspective` container mapped to the user's vertical scroll, scrolling down the page visually translates to walking *forward* down a long, dimly lit concrete hallway. The color palette is composed of rich charcoals, slate grays, and warm, volumetric spotlighting that highlights the "artworks" hanging on the walls. 

At the entrance of the gallery, the user faces a massive, freestanding concrete partition. On it, in elegant, architectural sans-serif typography, is the developer’s name: **Geva Kipper**. Just below, styled like a museum curation statement, reads a single sentence: "Currently engineering solutions at Google." Beneath this is an ever-shifting, subtly glowing subtitle: "This exhibition is re-designed each day by AI and is thus ever-changing." As the user scrolls, they walk past this partition and enter the main exhibition hall.

The projects are displayed as large, framed, high-resolution canvases alternating on the left and right walls of the 3D hallway. As the camera approaches each artwork, a hovering, interactive museum placard fades in next to it. 
Exhibit 1 (Left): **GlglStats**. The canvas features a vivid visualization of repetition patterns in Israeli pop songs. The placard explains the LZ77 compression Python project and features two sleek buttons: one linking to the live site, and another titled "View Artist Talk," linking to the PyData TLV YouTube presentation.
Exhibit 2 (Right): **Ahuzat Dibuk**. The canvas displays glowing heat-maps of Tel-Aviv parking occupancy. The placard details the Python scraping project, with interactive "Press Archive" links pointing to the Channel 13 and Mako news stories, and a "Community Discussion" link pointing to the original Secret Tel-Aviv Facebook post.
Exhibit 3 (Left): **2D Part Orienting**. The canvas holds an abstract, geometric polygon layout. The placard details the C++ open-source computational geometry implementation for push-plan orientation, featuring a link to the TAU CGL project page.
Exhibit 4 (Right): **The Turing Jest**. Framed like a retro-futuristic battle arena, the placard explains the LMSYS-like competition where LLMs are rated on pun creation and explanation, alongside a link to the live arena.

Continuing down the hallway:
Exhibit 5 (Left): **Rhymes of Israel**. The artwork is styled like a comedic, highschool yearbook page. The text explains the LLM Chrome extension that transforms Israeli news headlines into rhymes, featuring a button that links directly to the Chrome Web Store.
Exhibit 6 (Right): **Hatul Basak**. The canvas shows a web of Wikipedia-style trivia paths. The placard describes the Hebrew port of catfishing.net, its daily AI-generated challenges, and user-themed puzzles, linking to the playable game.
Exhibit 7 (Center): At the end of the long hallway sits a final, standalone masterpiece. It is a recursive, "Droste effect" mirror frame representing the **Portfolio Website** itself. The placard playfully acknowledges the meta nature of the project and provides a self-reference link to geekip.dev.

Behind Exhibit 7 is the "Exit / Guestbook" lounge area. Suspended from the dark ceiling are three sleek, glowing neon icons acting as exit signs: GitHub, LinkedIn, and Facebook, linking directly to gevak, gevakip, and geva.kipper respectively. Finally, projected onto the floor in crisp, glowing white monospace typography, is a credit line reading: "Exhibition Curated & Constructed by an AI Model." Directly beneath it is a live, ticking digital stopwatch, dynamically calculating and displaying the exact hours, minutes, and seconds elapsed since 2026-08-06 01:55:35.
