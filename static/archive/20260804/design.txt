**Global Layout and Interaction Mechanics**
The website is a single-page, full-screen interactive experience. The foundational layer is a sprawling, high-resolution, ultra-wide 2.5D landscape—a seamless blend of nature and futuristic cityscapes created using layered SVG and canvas elements to achieve a deep parallax effect. The user's screen acts as the lens of a pair of binoculars. This is achieved via a fixed, screen-filling CSS overlay featuring a soft, dark vignette shaped like two overlapping circles. The standard mouse cursor is replaced by a minimalist, glowing reticle (crosshair). As the user moves their mouse, the background landscape smoothly pans in the opposite direction, simulating the act of scanning the horizon. 

**Header and Overlay UI**
Anchored to the top center of the screen, sitting perfectly within the upper bridge of the binocular vignette, is a sleek, frosted-glass (glassmorphism) HUD overlay. It displays the primary typography in an elegant, modern sans-serif font. The main header reads: "Geva Kipper", followed by a refined, single-sentence subheader: "Currently shaping the future of technology as a Software Engineer at Google." Just below this is a smaller, italicized, dynamically glowing subtitle: "This digital landscape is uniquely re-designed and generated each day by AI—you are experiencing today's ever-changing iteration."

**The Exploration and Zooming Experience**
Hidden across the panoramic landscape are eight distinct, animated visual landmarks representing the projects and links. When the user's reticle sweeps over a landmark, a subtle lock-on animation occurs, and the landmark glows. Clicking triggers a cinematic JavaScript-powered zoom: the landscape scales up massively, the edges blur via CSS backdrop filters, and a beautiful, high-tech "Project File" modal slides into the center of the binocular view. A "Zoom Out" button in the corner returns the user to the panoramic scan.

**The Eight Hidden Landmarks and Modals**
1. **GlglStats:** Hidden as a futuristic radio tower emitting visible, pulsing frequency waves. Clicking zooms in to reveal a modal titled 'GlglStats'. The description explains it is a Python project measuring the repetitiveness of Israeli pop-chart songs based on LZ77 compression, complete with data visualizations of famous songs. It includes a sleek button linking to `https://glglstats.geekip.dev` and an embedded thumbnail linking to the PyData TLV presentation at `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jrKAtOsj1Lo`.
2. **Ahuzat Dibuk:** Hidden as a dense, glowing parking structure with a thermal heat-map overlay over its roof. The modal details a Python scraping and data visualization project collecting Tel-Aviv car parking lot occupancy statistics and generating heat-maps for residents. It features an array of press links styled as news clippings pointing to Channel 13 (`https://13tv.co.il/item/news/domestic/internal/parking-902956407/`), Mako (`https://www.mako.co.il/nexter-news/Article-ae43964b891bf71027.htm`), and the original Facebook publication (`https://www.facebook.com/groups/secrettelaviv/posts/10159636301195943/`).
3. **2D Part Orienting:** Hidden as a glowing, shifting geometric polygon floating above a robotic assembly line in a valley. The modal presents a C++ implementation within an open-source computational geometry library, detailing an efficient algorithm for constructing a 'push plan' to orient polygons. A technical blueprint-style button links to the code and info at `https://www.cgl.cs.tau.ac.il/projects/2d-part-orienting/`.
4. **The Turing Jest:** Hidden as an ancient Roman colosseum where two robotic figures stand on a stage holding comedy masks. The modal explains it is an LMSYS-like arena where LLMs compete at making and explaining puns, rated by human users. An inviting "Enter Arena" button links to `https://jest.geekip.dev`.
5. **Rhymes of Israel:** Hidden as a holographic newspaper stand caught in the wind, with headlines morphing into rhyming text. The modal introduces a Chrome extension using an LLM to rewrite Israeli news headlines into comedic rhymes in the style of a high school yearbook. A Chrome Web Store badge links to `https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/eainginjgdkpgolpjniijdihfbeeeklb`.
6. **Hatul Basak:** Hidden as a giant, mysterious cat statue made of interlocking puzzle pieces next to a digital library. The modal presents a Hebrew port of catfishing.net, an AI-powered Wikipedia-based trivia game featuring daily challenges and user-generated themed puzzles. A playful button links to play and create puzzles at `https://hatulbasak.geekip.dev`.
7. **The Meta Portfolio:** Hidden as an artist's easel facing the user, displaying a miniature version of the exact landscape the user is currently exploring. The modal playfully acknowledges the meta nature of the project—a website presenting the website being built. A self-referential button links to `https://geekip.dev`.
8. **Social Connections:** Hidden on the highest mountain peak as a sleek communication satellite dish transmitting signals to the stars. The modal is an elegant contact card featuring minimalist icons and links to GitHub (`github.com/gevak`), LinkedIn (`linkedin.com/in/gevakip`), and Facebook (`facebook.com/geva.kipper`).

**Footer and Timekeeping**
Anchored to the bottom center of the binocular HUD, designed with a tactical, monospaced digital aesthetic, is the footer. It features the text: "Design architected by Gemini AI." Next to this is a live, ticking JavaScript stopwatch. It calculates the exact elapsed time (in hours, minutes, and seconds) since the target date of `2026-08-04 01:52:31`, continuously updating every second to emphasize the passage of time within this ever-evolving digital landscape.
