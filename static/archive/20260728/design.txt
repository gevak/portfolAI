**Global Concept and Mechanics**
The core interaction of the single-page application is an "Inverted Ascent" driven by scrolling. While the user's mouse wheel scrolls *down*, the viewport camera translates *upwards*, lifting the perspective from the ground level up into the exosphere. The page is vertically divided into exactly 28 distinct, full-screen atmospheric layers. As the user scrolls, a fixed, minimalist side-navigation (01/28 to 28/28) tracks their altitude. The layers transition visually using CSS gradient shifts and GSAP ScrollTrigger parallax effects—starting from warm morning earth tones, passing through fluffy tropospheric clouds (HTML5 Canvas volumetric clouds), rising past high-altitude weather balloons in the stratosphere, auroras in the thermosphere, and finally ending in the starry deep-space of the exosphere. The content boxes are housed in semi-transparent, frosted "Glassmorphism" cards with soft, glowing borders that adapt to the ambient color of their current sky layer.

**Layer 1: The Launchpad (Header)**
Set against a soft, sunlit ground-to-sky parallax background. The typography is elegant and modern (e.g., Space Grotesk). The main headline reads "Geva Kipper" in large, crisp typography. Immediately beneath it is a single, clean sentence: "Software Engineer at Google, [insert single sentence describing current work]." Below this, a subtitle utilizes a monospaced font with an active JS typewriter and glitch effect: "Warning: This website is re-designed each day by AI and is thus ever-changing." A subtle, floating mouse icon prompts the user to "Scroll down into the sky."
